# Founder pathway in AI safety

Detailed guidance for users considering founding an organisation in AI safety. Read this for any founder, founding-team, or "what could I build" question. ## What founding in AI safety actually looks like

Founding in AI safety spans several distinct shapes:

- **Technical research nonprofit** — small (often 3–15 people) research org focused on a specific safety agenda (e.g. control, interpretability, evaluations, governance research). Typically funded by safety-aligned funders (Coefficient Giving, SFF, individual donors). Founder usually has research credibility plus operational capability.
- **Applied safety tooling startup** — for-profit or PBC building tools that contribute to safety (evaluation infrastructure, interpretability tooling, hardware security, agent monitoring, etc.). Typically VC-funded, with safety-aligned investors. Founder usually has technical depth.
- **Fieldbuilding org** — nonprofit running programmes, training, community infrastructure, career support, conferences. Founder usually has organisation-running experience and a thesis about what part of the talent pipeline is broken.
- **Evaluation org** — focused on testing and evaluating AI systems. Sits between research nonprofit and applied startup depending on commercial vs purely-research framing.
- **Advocacy or policy org** — nonprofit focused on shaping public discourse, policy advocacy, or specific campaigns. Founder usually has comms, policy, or political organising background.
- **Geographic fieldbuilding org** — building AI safety capacity in a specific country or region not yet well-served (Latin America, India, parts of Asia, Africa). Founder usually has local context plus AI safety engagement.

The founder profile that fits varies hugely across these. Be specific about which one the user is considering before suggesting incubators or routes.

## What makes a credible founder profile in AI safety

Across founder shapes, the recurring elements in successful AI safety founder profiles:

- **A specific thesis about what to build and why.** Not "I want to start an AI safety org" but "I think there's a missing piece of work in X, here's why no one else is doing it well, here's what I'd build, here's why I'm the right person." Funders and incubators read for specificity.
- **Demonstrated execution capability.** The founder has built or scaled something before — not necessarily a company. Founding a programme, running a team, shipping a hard project, building a community. The signal is "would actually do the thing."
- **Real engagement with AI safety.** Mission alignment that's testable, inside-view models of the field, an existing network of relationships in safety. Founders who arrive cold without context typically get turned down by safety-specific incubators.
- **Co-founder fit (if applicable).** Most incubators heavily weight co-founder dynamics. Pre-idea programmes (Catalyze, BlueDot Incubator) include co-founder matching specifically because they know how much this matters.
- **Willingness to take on the founder job.** Not "I want to lead an org" but "I want to spend the next 3–5 years doing the all-of-the-job work of starting and scaling a thing." Many pivoters discover they want senior operator roles, not founder roles. This is a useful filter.

If the user is missing one or more of these, name the gap and discuss whether founding is the right move or whether joining an existing org as a senior operator is a better fit.

## The incubator landscape

Several distinct programmes exist. They serve different founder shapes and have different bars.

### Catalyze Impact

**Who it serves:** Non-technical and technical founders of AI safety orgs (both nonprofit and for-profit). Pre-idea and idea-stage. Particularly accessible to generalists with strong execution backgrounds.

**What they provide:** Co-founder matching, mentorship, network access, seed funding pathway, stipend.

**Track record:** ~15 orgs incubated to date. Alumni include Luthien, TamperSec, Timaeus, Coordinal, Wiser Human, Cadenza Labs, Groundless, Aintelope, Netholabs. Most have raised 6 or 7 figures.

**Verify at:** catalyze-impact.org — cohort timing, programme structure, application windows, and location all change. Check the current site.

### BlueDot Incubator Week

**Who it serves:** Technically capable people working in or adjacent to AI safety. Short, intensive format. Funding available for the strongest pitches.

**What they provide:** Short intensive programme in London with funding for selected teams.

**Verify at:** bluedot.org — programme details, dates, application process. Sits within BlueDot's wider career-support offering.

### Seldon Lab

**Who it serves:** Technical founders specifically — explicitly looking for ex-frontier-lab engineers and people with deep technical expertise (cryptography, distributed systems, AI security infrastructure). **Not a generalist founder pathway.** Do not recommend Seldon to a non-technical pivoter looking to found.

**What they provide:** SF-based accelerator, investment per startup, focus on AI security infrastructure.

**Track record:** Pilot batch backed companies that sold to xAI and Anthropic; ongoing batches focus on AI security infrastructure.

**Verify at:** seldonlab.com — batch timing, focus areas, RFS.

### Halcyon Futures

**Who it serves:** Builders working on AI safety, security, and assurance. Operates as both nonprofit incubator and VC fund.

**What they provide:** Project incubation, grants, investments. Has been associated with helping launch several AI safety companies.

**Verify at:** Halcyon Futures' own site for current programmes and what they're funding.

### Fifty Years

**Who it serves:** Scientists and engineers wanting to build startups in AI safety and adjacent areas.

**Verify at:** fiftyyears.com.

### Other entry points

- **AE Studio:** Has expressed interest in seeding neglected safety approaches, including ideas that take business-shape.
- **D/acc residency:** For builders working on "decentralized, democratic, differential, defensive acceleration."
- **Constellation Incubator:** Supports pre-idea entrepreneurs and early-stage orgs.
- **BlueDot AGI Strategy Course:** Functions as an incubation course; graduates can apply for funding or attend an incubation week.
- **Founders Pledge / Charity Entrepreneurship adjacent:** Some founder support exists through EA-adjacent infrastructure.

For all of these: provide the canonical URL and instruct the user to verify current offerings. None of these run identical programmes year over year.

## What product-shaped tech founders can build

For users with product or tech backgrounds asking "what kind of orgs can I found?", a useful menu:

**Evaluation tooling.** Building infrastructure that AI labs, AISIs, and enterprises use to evaluate AI systems for safety properties. Real commercial market, growing. Examples in this space: METR-shaped work, Apollo Research adjacent, the Seldon Lab portfolio.

**Interpretability tooling.** Tools that make AI model internals legible — circuit tracing, activation steering, mechanistic interpretability infrastructure. Goodfire is the high-profile example.

**Agent monitoring and oversight.** As AI agents proliferate, infrastructure to monitor, audit, and constrain them is in demand. Andon Labs has been in this space.

**Hardware security and verifiable compute.** GPU monitoring, compute attestation, secure inference. Examples include Lucid Computing (in the Seldon portfolio).

**AI safety for enterprise.** Tooling that helps enterprises deploy AI safely — guardrails, monitoring, red-teaming services, governance tools. Shay Yahal's stealth company has been in this space.

**Adversarial / red-teaming products.** Productised red-teaming for AI systems. 0Labs and similar.

**AI safety for specific high-stakes domains.** Healthcare, defence, financial systems, infrastructure — building safety-specific tooling for specific high-consequence sectors.

**Provenance, authenticity, and content verification.** Especially in the wake of widespread synthetic media, demand for systems that verify what's authentic.

**AI safety insurance and risk quantification.** Quantifying AI risk for insurance markets and enterprises. Exona Lab (Seldon alumnus) has worked in this space.

For all of these, the framing matters: the user needs a specific thesis about what gap they'd address and why they're the right founder, not just "I'd build something in this space."

## When founding probably isn't the right move

Be willing to redirect users away from founding when it's a poor fit. Honest signals:

- **No specific thesis about what to build.** "I want to found something in AI safety" without a specific problem they're motivated to solve usually doesn't lead anywhere good. Better: target senior operator roles at existing orgs where they can develop a thesis.
- **No execution track record.** Founders with no prior building or running experience face a steep ramp. Better: get execution experience as a senior employee at an existing org first.
- **Pure intellectual interest without operational appetite.** Some pivoters discover they want to think about safety, not run a thing. Researcher, analyst, or advisor roles fit better.
- **Funding pressure as primary motivation.** If the user is considering founding because they think it's faster than getting hired, the math usually doesn't work — founding is harder, slower, and lower-paid than most pivoters anticipate.

When redirecting, name the adjacent path that fits better (e.g. "Given X, I think a senior operator role at an established field-building org would let you build a thesis faster than founding cold").

## Sequencing: when to apply to incubators

For users serious about founding, a reasonable rough sequence:

1. **Get real AI safety context** — BlueDot's course, sustained reading, conversations with people in the field. Usually months, not weeks.
2. **Develop a thesis** — what would you actually build, and why is it neglected? Write it down. Share with experienced operators in the space for feedback.
3. **Test the thesis cheaply** — talk to potential users, customers, or beneficiaries. Validate that the problem is real and that you're the right person to address it.
4. **Apply to incubators** that match your founder shape (Catalyze for generalist or technical, Seldon for high-bar technical, BlueDot Incubator for short intensive, others as relevant).
5. **In parallel, talk to safety-aligned funders or investors** about your thesis. Route to **AI Safety Funding** if it is loaded for who to talk to; if not loaded, direct them to aisafety.com/funding.

This sequence is approximate. Some users will compress it (experienced founders with clear theses can apply to incubators directly). Others will spend longer on context-building. Don't be prescriptive — describe the shape and let them adapt.
